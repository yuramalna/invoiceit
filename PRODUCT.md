# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Hours is for one independent professional managing several part-time client engagements. It is used at a desk for focused administration and in shorter moments during the day to start, stop, and correct time tracking. The primary user needs one private system of record for clients, projects, rates, time entries, and invoices.

## Product Purpose

Hours turns a fragmented set of engagements into an orderly billing workflow. It tracks time against a client and project, applies the correct rate, shows what remains unbilled, and produces a clear invoice with the underlying time log. Success means the user can account for a working day quickly and close a billing period without rebuilding the story in a spreadsheet.

## Positioning

A private, self-hosted work ledger that moves cleanly from timer to invoice.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, and bookish. The product speaks plainly, uses sentence case, and avoids marketing language, celebration, emoji, and unnecessary reassurance. It should feel like a well-kept notebook with the confidence and density of a professional instrument.

## Anti-references

Hours should not resemble a generic SaaS dashboard, a gamified productivity app, or a glossy fintech product. Avoid decorative gradients, large soft shadows, bubbly cards, display serifs, illustration, frosted surfaces, and color used without meaning. Terracotta is reserved for an actively running timer.

## Design Principles

- Make the current task obvious: the running timer is always the first object on Today.
- Let numbers do the work: durations, rates, totals, and counts remain aligned and easy to audit.
- Preserve the paper trail: entries, projects, rates, invoices, and exports should feel like one continuous ledger.
- Keep private infrastructure understandable: SQLite persistence, backups, synchronization, and future desktop integration should be visible without becoming the product’s personality.
- Prefer familiar, reversible actions over clever interaction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface supports keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, readable contrast, semantic labels, and touch-safe controls. Information is never conveyed by client or status color alone.
